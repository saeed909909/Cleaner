How to use this book

This book explains how modern AI works, from the smallest building block (a single artificial neuron) up to the large language models people chat with every day, and the hardware they run on. It assumes no maths beyond multiplying and adding, and no programming.

The aim is for you to keep up with the vocabulary. When someone says “it's a 70B mixture-of-experts model, quantized to 4 bits, with a 128k context window”, you should be able to picture every part of that sentence. By the end you will.

Read it in order, the first time

Each chapter builds on the ones before it. Part I explains neurons and learning; everything after that is built from those two ideas. Later, use the contents, glossary and index to jump around.

Look out for the boxes

Analogy

Orange boxes compare a hard idea to something familiar. Analogies are never perfect, so each one helps you picture the idea, not replace it.

In plain words

Blue boxes restate the key point of a section in a sentence or two.

Good to know

Purple boxes hold extra detail, history, or a common misunderstanding.

In your projects

Green boxes connect the idea to the small AI projects built alongside this book: Tiny AI, the 100-parameter network that grows neurons and learns from a teacher, and Tiny Writer, the miniature GPT that learns to write from Moby-Dick.

Words in colour

When an important term is first explained, it appears in blue bold. Every one of those terms is in the glossary at the back with a short definition, and in the index with the page where it is explained.

A note on facts: AI moves quickly. The ideas in this book are stable and have been for years. Specific products, prices and records change every few months; where this book gives them, treat them as examples from late 2026, not permanent truths.
